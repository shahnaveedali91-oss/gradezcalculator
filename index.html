How to Calculate My Grade?

If you want to calculate your grade manually, you first need to understand how your teacher or institution calculates the overall course result.

There are two common approaches: weighted grading and unweighted grading.

A weighted grading system gives different assignments different levels of importance. For example, a final exam might contribute 40% of your course grade, while homework contributes only 10%.

An unweighted grading system treats every assignment equally, regardless of its importance.

Before calculating your grade, check your course syllabus to determine which method applies.

Grade Calculation Formula

For courses that use weighted assignments, the general formula is:

Weighted Grade = Σ (Assignment Score × Assignment Weight) ÷ Total Assignment Weight

Here is what each part means:

  • Assignment Score: The percentage you earned on an assignment.
  • Assignment Weight: The percentage that assignment contributes to your course grade.
  • Total Assignment Weight: The sum of the weights included in your calculation.

If all assignment weights add up to 100%, you can simply add their weighted contributions to find your overall grade. If you are calculating a current grade using only completed work, divide by the total weight of the completed work included in the calculation. Gradebook policies differ on whether ungraded work is excluded or treated as zero, so check your instructor's settings and syllabus.

Example of Calculating Your Overall Grade

Suppose you are taking a college course with four graded components. Your instructor has assigned different weights to each component.

AssignmentYour ScoreWeightGrade Contribution
Homework92%20%18.40
Quizzes88%15%13.20
Midterm Exam78%25%19.50
Final Exam85%40%34.00
Total100%85.10%

Now multiply each assignment score by its weight:

  • Homework: 92 × 0.20 = 18.40
  • Quizzes: 88 × 0.15 = 13.20
  • Midterm: 78 × 0.25 = 19.50
  • Final Exam: 85 × 0.40 = 34.00

Add all four contributions:

18.40 + 13.20 + 19.50 + 34.00 = 85.10%

Your overall course grade is 85.10%.

This example demonstrates why assignment weights matter. Even though your homework score is higher than your midterm score, the midterm contributes more to your final result because it carries greater weight.

How Does a Weighted Grading Calculator Work?

A weighted grading calculator determines your course grade by considering the importance of each assignment.

Unlike a simple average, where every score has equal value, weighted grading gives more influence to assignments with higher percentages.

For example, imagine your course uses the following grading structure:

The benefit of weighted grading is that it reflects the grading structure established by your instructor rather than treating every assignment as equally important.

If you are unsure about an assignment's weight, check your course syllabus before entering it. The University of Illinois explains that instructors may assign different weights to graded components such as exams, papers, projects, and quizzes. See its guidance on assigning course grades.

How to Calculate Your Grade Without Weights

Not every class uses a weighted grading system. Some instructors calculate grades by treating every assignment equally.

In this situation, you can use a simple average formula.

Average Grade = Sum of All Scores ÷ Number of Assignments

For example, suppose you receive the following scores:

AssignmentScore
Assignment 185%
Assignment 290%
Assignment 378%
Assignment 492%
Total345

Add all your scores:

85 + 90 + 78 + 92 = 345

Divide the total by the number of assignments:

345 ÷ 4 = 86.25%

Your average grade is 86.25%.

This method works when every assignment has equal value. If your instructor gives exams, projects, or homework different weights, use the weighted calculation instead.

How to Calculate a Grade From Points

Some teachers and educational institutions calculate grades using points rather than assignment percentages.

For example, an exam might be worth 100 points, a homework assignment 25 points, and a project 50 points.

To calculate your percentage grade, use this formula:

Grade Percentage = (Points Earned ÷ Total Possible Points) × 100

Consider the following example:

You earned 72 points out of 80 possible points on an examination.

72 ÷ 80 × 100 = 90%

Your examination percentage is 90%.

Students in the United Kingdom may encounter different grading systems depending on their educational level and qualification.

Unlike the commonly used A to F system in the United States, UK schools and universities use several grading structures.

For example, GCSE qualifications in England use a numerical grading system from 9 to 1, with U representing an ungraded result. This system replaced the previous A* to G scale in 2017. What Is an Easy Grader?

An easy grader, sometimes called an EZ grader, is a tool that helps teachers and students calculate test percentages using the number of correct or incorrect answers.

It is particularly useful when grading quizzes, classroom tests, and multiple-choice examinations.

Instead of calculating every percentage manually, use this formula:

Test Grade = (Correct Answers ÷ Total Questions) × 100

For example, suppose your examination contains 25 questions and you answer 22 correctly.

22 ÷ 25 × 100 = 88%

Your test percentage is 88%.

If you know the number of incorrect answers instead, subtract that number from the total questions first.

For example:

  • Total questions: 25
  • Incorrect answers: 3
  • Correct answers: 25 − 3 = 22
  • Percentage: 22 ÷ 25 × 100 = 88%

A quick grade calculator or easy grader is most useful when every question has the same value. If questions carry different point values, calculate using the actual points earned and possible.

How to Calculate the Grade You Need on Your Final Exam

One of the most common questions students ask is how much they need to score on their final examination to achieve a particular course grade.

The answer depends on your current grade and the percentage weight of the final exam.

Use this formula:

Required Final Exam Score = (Target Grade − Current Grade × (1 − Final Exam Weight)) ÷ Final Exam Weight

Remember to express the final exam weight as a decimal.

Example: What Grade Do I Need to Get an A?

Suppose your current grade on completed coursework is 85%, your target is 90%, and your final examination contributes 30% of your overall grade.

Your completed coursework accounts for 70% of the final grade.

Using the formula:

Required Score = (90 − (85 × 0.70)) ÷ 0.30

Required Score = (90 − 59.5) ÷ 0.30

Required Score = 30.5 ÷ 0.30

Required Final Exam Score = 101.67%

This means you would need approximately 101.67% on the final exam to achieve your 90% target under these assumptions.

Without extra credit or another grading adjustment, that target is not mathematically achievable.

You can use the same calculation to determine the score required to pass a course or reach a different target percentage.

Common Grade Calculation Mistakes to Avoid

Even when using a grading calculator, incorrect inputs can produce misleading results. Pay attention to the following mistakes when calculating your academic performance.

  1. Using the Wrong Assignment Weight
    Always check your syllabus before entering assignment weights. If your final exam is worth 40%, entering 30% will change your overall result.
  2. Treating Every Assignment Equally
    Do not use a simple average when your course follows weighted grading. A final examination worth 40% should have more influence than a homework assignment worth 5%.
  3. Including Ungraded Assignments as Zero
    An assignment that has not been graded is not necessarily a zero. Some gradebooks exclude ungraded work when displaying a current grade, while others may use different settings. Check your instructor's policy before entering missing or ungraded work as zero.
  4. Rounding Your Scores Too Early
    Keep decimal values during your calculations. For example, 89.67% should not automatically be changed to 90% before the remaining calculations are complete. Your institution may have its own rounding policy.
  5. Forgetting Extra Credit and Dropped Grades
    Some instructors allow extra credit or remove the lowest assignment score. These adjustments can affect your final result. Apply them according to your course grading policy rather than assuming every class handles them the same way.
  6. Confusing Your Course Grade With Your GPA
    Your course grade represents your performance in an individual class. Your GPA combines grade points from multiple courses, usually taking credit hours into account. A course percentage and a cumulative GPA are therefore different measurements.
